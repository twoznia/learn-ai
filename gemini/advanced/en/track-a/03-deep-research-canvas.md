## 🧠 Theory (4 min)

Beyond chat, the Gemini app has two power features that make you dramatically more productive — no code required:

- **Deep Research** — you give a topic; Gemini **browses many sources**, then compiles a structured, cited **report**. It's like handing research to an assistant who reports back.
- **Canvas** — an interactive workspace where Gemini drafts a **document or code** you can edit side-by-side and refine, instead of scrolling a chat.

Both may depend on your plan/rollout. If one isn't available, the *skills* still transfer — you'll recognize the equivalent when it appears.

---

## 🛠️ Practice (9 min)

### Part 1 — Deep Research

1. In the Gemini app, look for a **Deep Research** option (a tool/mode near the prompt box or model picker).
2. Give it a real research task with scope:

```text
Research the best budget standing desks available in Europe right now.
Compare 4-5 options on price, height range, stability, and warranty.
Give me a short recommendation table and cite every source.
```

3. Gemini will show a **plan**, then browse and compile. Review the plan before it runs — you can refine scope.
4. When the report arrives, **click through the citations**. A research report is only as good as its sources.

> No Deep Research on your plan? Approximate it: turn on web search (beginner Lesson 5) and ask for "a comparison table with sources," then follow up to deepen each row.

### Part 2 — Canvas

1. Start a task that produces a **document or code**, and open it in **Canvas** (look for a Canvas option when a long draft appears):

```text
Draft a one-page project proposal for a community garden. Put it in Canvas so I can edit it.
```

2. In Canvas, edit directly and ask Gemini for targeted changes:

```text
Make the budget section a table. Shorten the intro to 3 sentences. Add a timeline.
```

3. Notice you're **iterating on a living document**, not re-reading a chat. This is far better for anything you'll actually ship.

### Part 3 — Combine them

The power workflow: **Deep Research → Canvas**.

```text
Take the research report above and turn it into a one-page buyer's guide in Canvas,
with a recommendation box at the top and a comparison table below.
```

Research gathers the facts; Canvas shapes them into a deliverable.

---

## 🧭 When to use which

| Feature | Use for |
|---------|---------|
| **Deep Research** | Multi-source questions, comparisons, "what's the current best…" |
| **Canvas** | Documents/code you'll edit and keep |
| **Both** | Research → polished deliverable |
| Plain chat | Quick questions, drafts, brainstorming |

> ⚠️ Deep Research still cites sources that can be wrong — verify anything you'll act on, just like any AI output.

---

## ✅ Checkpoint

- [ ] You ran a Deep Research task (or the web-search approximation) and checked its citations.
- [ ] You drafted and iterated on something in Canvas (or an equivalent long-form draft).
- [ ] You combined research into a Canvas deliverable.

---

## 🎯 Homework

Pick a real decision you're facing (a purchase, a plan, a trip). Run Deep Research on it, then turn the result into a one-page summary in Canvas that you'd actually share.

---

## 💡 Key takeaways

- **Deep Research** compiles cited, multi-source reports — review the plan and the sources.
- **Canvas** lets you iterate on a living document/code instead of scrolling chat.
- The strongest workflow is **Research → Canvas** for a polished deliverable.
