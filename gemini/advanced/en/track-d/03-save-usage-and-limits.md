# D3 — Save Usage & Manage Your Limits

⏱️ **15 minutes** · Track: 🅳 Subscription Power · Needs: a Google account

## 🧠 Theory (5 min)

Your Google AI plan gives you a **generous but not unlimited** amount of usage, and the most capable model has **tighter limits** than the fast one. Limits refill over time (and higher plans give higher ceilings). You don't pay per message — but if you burn through, you wait or drop to a lighter model.

Everything you send **and** everything Gemini generates counts. So the two levers are:

1. **Send less unnecessary stuff** (relevant context, the right model).
2. **Ask for less unnecessary output** (be specific about length and format).

Efficiency isn't cramming — it's **not wasting your allowance** on things you didn't need.

---

## 🛠️ Practice (9 min)

### Step 1 — Know your limits

Check **Settings / your plan page** for how usage works and when it resets. Notice the **most-capable model** usually has a separate, smaller allowance than the fast one.

### Step 2 — Right-size the model

Match the model to the task (from D1):

- **Flash (fast)** for quick or routine work — light on your allowance.
- **Pro (capable)** only when the task genuinely needs deep reasoning.

Running a simple lookup on the fast model instead of the capable one is a free win.

### Step 3 — Keep context lean

From D2: a long chat re-reads everything each turn. Concretely:
- **One chat = one task**; start fresh for new topics.
- Paste the **relevant part**, not the whole document.
- Attach files instead of pasting huge blocks when you can.

### Step 4 — Ask for the output you want

Unbounded answers waste your allowance. Steer length and shape:

```text
Answer in 3 bullet points, one line each. No preamble.
```
```text
Just give me the final formula — no explanation.
```
```text
Summarize in under 100 words.
```

### Step 5 — Reuse instead of regenerating

- Save good prompts (Track A2's library) so you don't re-craft them.
- Put standing context in a **Gem** so you never re-paste it.
- If you already have a good answer, **edit** it yourself rather than regenerating the whole thing.

### Step 6 — Batch small things

Instead of ten tiny back-and-forths, ask for several related things in **one** well-structured message. Fewer turns = less re-reading of the growing chat.

---

## 🧩 Usage-saving cheat sheet

| Habit | Why it saves |
|-------|-------------|
| Right-size the model | The fast model uses less of your allowance |
| One chat = one task | No stale context re-read every turn |
| Paste the relevant part | Less input to process |
| Specify length/format | No wasted output |
| Use Gems for standing context | Stop re-pasting background |
| Batch related asks | Fewer turns, less re-reading |
| Edit instead of regenerate | Don't pay twice for the same answer |

## 🎯 Homework

Take a task you'd normally do in a long, messy chat. Redo it efficiently: right model, lean context, a length instruction, one batched message. Notice how much faster it is — that's allowance you saved (and the capable model's quota preserved for when you need it).

---

## 💡 Key takeaways

- Usage counts **both** what you send and what Gemini generates; the **capable model** has a tighter allowance — spend it deliberately.
- Right-size the **model**, keep **context lean**, and **specify** the output you want.
- **Gems** + saved prompts + editing (not regenerating) stop repeated waste.
