# D3 — Save Usage & Manage Your Limits

⏱️ **15 minutes** · Track: 🅳 Subscription Power · Needs: a ChatGPT account

---

## 🧠 Theory (5 min)

Your ChatGPT plan gives you a **generous but not unlimited** amount of usage, and the most capable "thinking" models have **tighter limits** (often a rolling message cap) than the fast one. When you hit a cap, you wait for it to reset or drop to a lighter model.

Everything you send **and** everything GPT generates counts. So the two levers are:

1. **Send less unnecessary stuff** (relevant context, the right model).
2. **Ask for less unnecessary output** (be specific about length and format).

Efficiency isn't cramming — it's **not wasting your allowance** on things you didn't need.

---

## 🛠️ Practice (9 min)

### Step 1 — Know your limits

Watch for the notice when you approach a cap on the thinking model, and check **Settings** for how your plan's limits work. The capable models reset on a rolling window separate from the fast one.

### Step 2 — Right-size the model

Match the model to the task (from D1):

- **Fast / everyday** for quick or routine work — light on your allowance.
- **Thinking / capable** only when the task genuinely needs deep reasoning.

Running a simple lookup on the fast model instead of the thinking one is a free win. (The **Auto** option does some of this for you.)

### Step 3 — Keep context lean

From D2: a long chat re-reads everything each turn. Concretely:
- **One chat = one task**; start fresh for new topics.
- Paste the **relevant part**, not the whole document.
- Attach files instead of pasting huge blocks when you can.

### Step 4 — Ask for the output you want

Unbounded answers waste your allowance. Steer length and shape:

```text
Answer in 3 bullet points, one line each. No preamble.
```
```text
Just give me the final SQL — no explanation.
```
```text
Summarize in under 100 words.
```

### Step 5 — Reuse instead of regenerating

- Save good prompts (Track A2's library) so you don't re-craft them.
- Put standing context in a **Project** or **Custom GPT** so you never re-paste it.
- If you already have a good answer, **edit** it yourself rather than regenerating the whole thing.

### Step 6 — Batch small things

Instead of ten tiny back-and-forths, ask for several related things in **one** well-structured message. Fewer turns = less re-reading of the growing chat.

---

## 🧩 Usage-saving cheat sheet

| Habit | Why it saves |
|-------|-------------|
| Right-size the model | The fast model uses less of your allowance |
| One chat = one task | No stale context re-read every turn |
| Paste the relevant part | Less input to process |
| Specify length/format | No wasted output |
| Use Projects / Custom GPTs | Stop re-pasting standing context |
| Batch related asks | Fewer turns, less re-reading |
| Edit instead of regenerate | Don't pay twice for the same answer |

---

## ✅ Checkpoint

- [ ] You know where to see your limits and that the thinking model has a tighter cap.
- [ ] You applied at least three usage-saving habits in a real chat.
- [ ] You can explain why both **input** and **output** count.

---

## 🎯 Homework

Take a task you'd normally do in a long, messy chat. Redo it efficiently: right model, lean context, a length instruction, one batched message. Notice how much faster it is — that's allowance saved (and the thinking model's cap preserved for when you need it).

---

## 💡 Key takeaways

- Usage counts **both** what you send and what GPT generates; the **thinking model** has a tighter cap — spend it deliberately.
- Right-size the **model**, keep **context lean**, and **specify** the output you want.
- **Projects / Custom GPTs** + saved prompts + editing (not regenerating) stop repeated waste.
