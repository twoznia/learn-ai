## 🧠 Theory (4 min)

A normal chat answers from what GPT already "knows." **Deep Research** is different: you give it a topic and it **browses many sources over several minutes**, then compiles a structured, **cited report** — like handing research to a diligent assistant who reports back with links.

When it shines:

- **Comparisons** — "the best X for Y, compared on price/features, with sources."
- **Current, verifiable questions** — where being up to date changes the answer.
- **"Look into this thoroughly"** — not a quick fact, but a real investigation.

The habit to build: use it when you need **depth and sources you can check** — then actually check them.

> Deep Research availability and monthly limits vary by plan. If you don't see it, a plain **web-search** prompt ("compare … and cite sources") is a lighter substitute.

## 🛠️ Practice (10 min)

### Step 1 — Start a Deep Research task

In ChatGPT, pick **Deep Research** from the tools menu (near the message box), then give it a scoped task:

```text
Research the best budget standing desks available now. Compare 4–5 options on
price, height range, stability, and warranty. Give a recommendation table and cite every source.
```

### Step 2 — Review the plan, then let it run

Deep Research often asks a **clarifying question** or shows a **plan** first. Answer it / approve it — this is where you set scope, so it doesn't wander. Then it works for a few minutes.

### Step 3 — Read the report *and* its citations

When it's done, you get a structured report with **citations**. Click through at least one source per key claim — a research report is only as good as its sources.

```text
Which of your sources is the most recent and most authoritative, and why?
```

### Step 4 — Refine the scope

If it went too broad or missed an angle, steer it:

```text
Focus only on desks under €300 available in the EU, and add a column for assembly time.
```

### Step 5 — Turn the research into a deliverable

Combine with Canvas (Track A3):

```text
Turn this into a one-page buyer's guide in Canvas: a recommendation box on top,
the comparison table below, and a "how we chose" note.
```

Research gathers the facts; Canvas shapes them into something you'd share.

### Step 6 — Know when *not* to use it

Deep Research is slow and uses more of your allowance. For a quick fact or a stable concept, a normal chat is better. Reserve it for **genuine investigations** where sources matter.

## 🧩 Which mode for which question

| Question | Use |
|----------|-----|
| "Compare current options for X, thoroughly" | **Deep Research** |
| "What's the latest on …?" (quick) | Web search |
| "Explain a stable concept" | Plain chat |
| "Summarize this file I uploaded" | Plain chat (no research) |
| Anything where being wrong costs you | Deep Research + **verify citations** |

> ⚠️ Cited doesn't mean correct — sources can be outdated or wrong. Verify anything you'll act on.

## ✅ Checkpoint

- [ ] You ran a Deep Research task (or the web-search substitute) with a scoped prompt.
- [ ] You answered its clarifying question / reviewed its plan.
- [ ] You clicked citations and confirmed they support the claims.
- [ ] You turned the result into a deliverable (e.g. a Canvas one-pager).

---

## 🎯 Homework

Pick a real decision you're researching (a purchase, a tool, a how-to). Run Deep Research with citations, verify two sources yourself, note where sources disagreed, and turn the result into a one-page summary you'd actually use.

---

## 💡 Key takeaways

- **Deep Research** compiles **cited, multi-source reports** — set scope via its clarifying question, and **check the sources**.
- Use it for real investigations; skip it for quick facts and stable concepts.
- Pair it with **Canvas** to turn research into a polished deliverable.
